USD to PKR from 1947 to 2026

The Pakistani rupee started at Rs 3.31 per dollar in 1947. It stayed fixed for long periods, then fell in steps after 1972 and much faster after 2018. By 2026 a dollar costs about 83 times as many rupees as at independence.

Key moments in the rupee's history

  • 1947–1955: Rs 3.31 per dollar. In 1949 Pakistan did not follow the sterling devaluation that India made.
  • August 1955: devalued to Rs 4.76, a rate that held until 1971.
  • May 1972: devalued to Rs 11, then set at Rs 9.90 from February 1973 until 1981.
  • January 1982: the fixed link to the dollar ended and a managed float began; the rupee fell steadily through the 1980s and 1990s.
  • 2008: a balance-of-payments crisis took the dollar from about Rs 62 to near Rs 80 within a year.
  • 2018–2019: from about Rs 110 to Rs 160 under the IMF programme and a market-based rate.
  • 2022–2023: the biggest fall in history, from about Rs 180 to above Rs 300 at the start of September 2023.
  • 2024–2026: a calmer period, mostly between Rs 276 and Rs 285 (highest monthly average Rs 284.13 in July 2025, lowest Rs 276.63 in November 2026).

Why the rupee keeps falling

The rupee weakens when Pakistan needs more dollars than it earns: imports (especially fuel) larger than exports, debt repayments, and low foreign exchange reserves. It steadies when remittances, exports, IMF and other inflows rise, and when inflation and interest rates are under control. For traders this matters because Gold in Pakistan is priced through the dollar: see the gold rate in Pakistan, which moves with both the world gold price and USD/PKR, and the gold rate history since 1947.

What was the dollar rate in Pakistan in 1947?

At independence one US dollar was about Rs 3.31. Pakistan kept that rate until August 1955, when the rupee was devalued to Rs 4.76.

What was the dollar rate in Pakistan in 2000, 2010 and 2018?

Yearly averages: Rs 53.65 in 2000, Rs 85.19 in 2010 and Rs 121.82 in 2018 (World Bank official rate).

The 2023 average was Rs 280.36. It was the sharpest fall of the rupee in its history; the dollar briefly went above Rs 300 in early September 2023.
