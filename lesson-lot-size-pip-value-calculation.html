Lesson notes

Why the same trade can lose $2 or $200

Two friends buy EURUSD at the same price with the stop loss 20 pips away. The market falls and both stops are hit. One loses $2, the other $200. Same chart, same entry, same stop: the only difference is the lot size. The first used 0.01 lot and the second 1.00 lot. This lesson shows exactly how that happens and how to choose the right size before every trade.

What is lot size in forex?

A rice shop sells a 100 kg sack, a 10 kg bag and a 1 kg packet. If the rate rises by 10 rupees a kilo, the sack owner gains 1,000 rupees, the bag owner 100 and the packet owner only 10. The rate moved the same for everyone; the gain depends on how much stock you hold. In forex that quantity is the lot: how many units of currency you buy or sell.

LotNameUnitsRice shop
1.00Standard lot100,000Sack
0.10Mini lot10,000Bag
0.01Micro lot1,000Packet

The units are always of the first (base) currency: one lot of EURUSD is 100,000 euros, paid for in dollars. At most brokers 0.01 is the smallest lot, and each size is ten times the one below it. In MT5 you rarely see the word lot: the Volume box in the order window is your lot size. Typing 1 instead of 0.01 makes the trade a hundred times bigger.

How does a small account open 100,000 euros? Through leverage and margin, which get their own module. For now remember: leverage decides how much money is blocked to open the trade; lot size decides how much you win or lose.

What is pip value?

A pip is a distance in price (0.0001 on EURUSD, see Lesson 2.1). Pip value is how much money changes in your account when price moves one pip. The formula is simple:

Pip value = pip size × units, and the answer is in the quote (second) currency. EURUSD, 1 lot: 0.0001 × 100,000 = 10, and the quote currency is the dollar, so 1 pip = $10 per lot.

The 10 pip rule (EURUSD and other pairs with USD second)

LotUnits10 pips1 pip
0.011,000$1$0.10
0.1010,000$10$1
1.00100,000$100$10

Ten times the lot, ten times the money. Back to the two friends: a 20 pip stop is double 10 pips, so 0.01 lot lost $2 and 1.00 lot lost $200 (0.10 lot would have lost $20). On a $500 account that 1 lot trade took 40% in one go. The rule works on every pair where the dollar comes second, such as GBPUSD and AUDUSD, whatever the price. Remember the line: the pip is distance, the lot is weight; money is the two multiplied.

USDJPY and cross pairs: pip value changes with price

On USDJPY a pip is 0.01. 0.01 × 100,000 = 1,000, but that is 1,000 yen, not dollars. With USDJPY around 159 (November 2026), 1,000 ÷ 159 ≈ $6.29 per pip per lot, not $10. The higher USDJPY goes, the smaller this value becomes. On pairs without the dollar, such as EURGBP, pip value is first in the quote currency (pounds) and then converted to dollars. MT5 does this conversion for you. The same pips on different pairs are not the same dollars.

Gold (XAUUSD) lot size and pip value

In gold, count dollars, not pips. One lot of gold is usually 100 ounces, so a $1 move in the gold price is $100 on 1 lot, $10 on 0.10 lot and $1 on 0.01 lot. Gold often moves $20 to $50 in a normal day, which is $20 to $50 even on 0.01 lot, while EURUSD on the same lot usually moves a few dollars. A lot that feels small on EURUSD is a much bigger risk on gold.

Check it yourself: contract size and tick value in MT5

In Market Watch right-click the symbol and open Specification.

  • EURUSD: contract size 100,000 (units of 1 lot), minimal volume 0.01, volume step 0.01. On a 5-digit broker the tick size is one pipette and the tick value is $1; ten pipettes make a pip, so $1 × 10 = $10 per pip per lot, the same as the formula.
  • Gold: contract size 100, tick size 0.01, tick value $1. A $1 move has 100 ticks, so $100 per lot.

Contract sizes differ between brokers, and on a cent account everything is in cents, so check your own broker once.

How to calculate lot size from risk (the professional way)

A beginner picks a lot first and hopes. A professional works backwards: first the maximum dollars he accepts losing, then the stop loss where the chart says it belongs, and only then the lot.

Lot = risk in dollars ÷ (stop loss in pips × pip value of 1 lot)

Example: $500 account, 1% risk = $5, stop loss 25 pips on EURUSD. 25 × $10 = $250, and $5 ÷ $250 = 0.02 lot. If the stop is hit, you lose only $5. Our free lot size calculator does this maths for forex and gold, and the QFT Trade Assistant does it inside MT5.

Small accounts: when even 0.01 lot is too big

A $100 account risking 1% ($1) with a 20 pip stop needs 1 ÷ (20 × 10) = 0.005 lot, but most brokers' smallest lot is 0.01, which doubles the risk to 2%. Don't shrink the stop loss to fit the lot: the chart decides where the stop goes. Either skip the trade or take the 2% risk knowingly. (A cent account is another way small traders size down; it comes up in the next lessons.) Read more in risk management in forex.

3 lot size mistakes to avoid

  1. Treating 0.1 and 0.01 as the same: one zero is ten times the risk.
  2. Using the same lot on every pair and on gold and assuming the risk is the same, when pip value and movement both differ.
  3. Doubling the lot after a loss to win it all back. This habit blows up more accounts than anything else. Increase the lot by calculation, not emotion.

1) In your demo MT5 open the specification of EURUSD, USDJPY and gold, and work out the pip value of each on 0.01 lot. 2) Open the free lot size calculator, enter a $500 account, 1% risk and a 25 pip stop loss. If you get 0.02, today's lesson is locked in. Write your USDJPY pip value in the YouTube comments. Next lesson: profit and loss in dollars, account currency and risk to reward.

Questions

How do you calculate pip value in forex?

Multiply the pip size by the number of units: pip value = pip size × units, in the quote currency. On EURUSD one lot is 100,000 units, so 0.0001 × 100,000 = $10 per pip. On 0.10 lot it is $1 and on 0.01 lot $0.10.

What is the 10 pip rule?

On EURUSD and other pairs where USD is the second currency, a 10 pip move is worth $1 on 0.01 lot, $10 on 0.10 lot and $100 on 1.00 lot. Ten times the lot means ten times the money.

How do I calculate lot size from my risk?

Lot = risk in dollars ÷ (stop loss in pips × pip value of 1 lot). With a $500 account, 1% risk ($5) and a 25 pip EURUSD stop: 5 ÷ (25 × 10) = 0.02 lot.

What is the pip value of USDJPY and gold?

USDJPY: 1,000 yen per pip per lot, about $6.29 at a price of 159, and it falls as USDJPY rises. Gold (100 oz contract): a $1 move is $100 on 1 lot, $10 on 0.10 lot and $1 on 0.01 lot.
