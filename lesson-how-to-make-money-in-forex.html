Lesson notes

Can $100 really make 10% a day?

You have probably seen the claim: invest $100 and earn 10% profit every day. Let's do the maths. If $100 grew by 10% a day and you reinvested the profit every day, after 30 days it would be about $1,744.94. After a full year the number would be bigger than all the personal wealth in the world. This is only a maths illustration, not a possible trading result. If it were that easy, everyone would be rich. So how is money really made in forex, and how is it lost?

Who is in the forex market?

About $9.6 trillion of currency changes hands every day (BIS Triennial Survey 2025). Six kinds of players share that market:

PlayerWho they areWhy they trade
Big banksThe heart of the market. They quote buy and sell prices all day.Profit from the spread and their own trading
Central banksFor example the State Bank of Pakistan or the Bank of Japan.Manage their own currency, not profit
Big institutionsHedge funds, investment funds and smaller banks; roughly half of all trading.Profit
Import and export companiesBuy and sell currency to pay for goods.Protect their business (hedging), not profit
Brokers and liquidity providersConnect ordinary traders to the big market.Spread, commission, swap, and sometimes your loss
Retail traders (us)Only a few percent of the whole market.Profit, but we are the smallest fish

How powerful is a central bank? On 15 January 2015 the Swiss National Bank removed its cap on the franc with one announcement. Within minutes the Swiss franc jumped about 30%. Many retail accounts were wiped out in a single day, and a London broker went bankrupt. Retail traders like us are the smallest fish in this ocean, which is why we learn to protect ourselves first.

How to earn money from forex trading: where profit comes from

In forex, money is made from the price difference. You buy EUR/USD and the price goes up: profit. It goes down: loss. If you sell, it is the opposite. (Lesson 1.1: What is forex trading? explains buy and sell from zero.)

On the other side of every trade there is someone: another trader, a bank or liquidity provider, or your own broker. One single person does not lose exactly what you win every time, but across the whole market money is not created out of thin air; it moves from one hand to another. So a winner's money comes from two places:

  1. The losses of traders who lose.
  2. Players who trade for their needs, not for profit, such as companies and central banks.

Your profit is not a gift from the market. It came out of someone's pocket, so to win you must make better decisions than others.

Where losers' money goes: 4 places

Where it goesHowRead more
1. Fees: spread, commission, swapThe spread puts every trade slightly in minus the moment it opens. Commission applies on some account types. Swap is charged (or sometimes paid) for holding a trade overnight.What is spread · What is swap
2. The broker itselfMany brokers do not send every trade to the big market; they take the other side themselves (often called a B-book). Then your loss becomes the broker's profit. This does not mean every broker cheats, but you should know it.Forex brokers guide
3. Better traders and institutionsBanks, funds and disciplined traders with better information, tools and risk control.Risk management
4. Outside the market: scams and signal sellersPaid signal groups, account managers and "guaranteed profit" schemes take money before a single trade is placed.Signal groups · Is forex a scam?

Islamic (swap-free) accounts

Many brokers offer an Islamic or swap-free account with no swap. But most of them charge a fixed admin fee for holding a trade overnight, either after a few days or from the first day. Some widen the spread a little, and some charge no admin fee at all. Not every broker works the same way, so read your broker's terms before opening an account. Module 4 covers broker and account types in detail.

Why the total is negative (the shovel sellers)

A fee is cut on every trade. So if you add up the profit and loss of all traders, the total is not zero: it is minus. Think of a cake where one slice is always cut away as a fee before anyone eats. The people who earn whether the market goes up or down are brokers, banks and signal sellers. In a gold rush, the one who earns most is the one selling shovels.

That is why regulated brokers in Europe and the UK must publish a risk warning on their own websites, and those warnings typically say about 60% to 80% of their retail accounts lose money trading CFDs.

The truth about a $50 or $100 account

Say you risk 2% of your account on each trade. On $100 that is $2, on $50 just $1. If a good trader makes 5% in a whole month, which is a very good result and not guaranteed, that is $5 on $100 and $2.50 on $50.

Can this run a household? No. This is where people go wrong: $5 feels too small, so they put 20% or 30% on one trade, and a few trades later the account is gone. A small account is for learning, not for earning.

How much should you risk per trade?

Risk means: if this trade goes wrong, how much of your account goes. You control it with a stop loss and the right lot size (Module 8 covers it fully; you can already try the free lot size calculator). A practical rule:

  • Small account ($50–$500): 2–3% per trade is acceptable, because the amount is small anyway and it is a learning account.
  • Bigger account: 1% or less, because every percent is now a big amount. 1% of $10,000 is $100.

10 losing trades in a row: 1% vs 3% vs 20% risk

Ten losses in a row can happen to any trader. Here is what it does to the account:

Risk per tradeAccount after 10 lossesTotal drawdown
1%$904.38−9.6%
3%$737.42−26.3%
20%$107.37−89.3%

At 1% you can recover. At 20% the account is practically finished (read more about drawdown and recovery).

Is forex trading profitable?

Yes, but only for traders who manage risk. That means three things: before every trade you decide the most you can lose, you have a daily and weekly loss limit, and you trade a tested plan. In the market, the one who earns is the one who survives first. Protect first, then earn.

Questions

Forex trading se paise kaise kamaye?

Forex mein paisa qeemat ke farq se banta hai: pair khareedein aur qeemat upar jaye, ya bechein aur neeche aaye. Lekin fees ki wajah se sab traders ka total minus hai, is liye sirf woh kamata hai jo har trade par risk fix rakhe (chhote account par 2-3%, bare par 1% ya kam), daily aur weekly limit rakhe aur tested plan par trade kare. Pehle bachao, phir kamao.

How do you make money from forex trading?

From the price difference: you buy a pair and it rises, or sell and it falls. The money comes from the losses of other traders and from players who trade for their needs, such as companies and central banks. After fees the total of all traders is negative, so only traders with better decisions and strict risk control end up in profit.

Is forex trading profitable?

It can be, but most retail traders lose. Regulated brokers' own risk warnings typically say about 60% to 80% of retail CFD accounts lose money. Profit is possible only with a tested plan, a fixed risk per trade and daily and weekly loss limits.

Can I make money in forex with $100?

You can learn with $100, but not earn a living. At 2% risk you lose at most $2 per trade, and a very good month of +5% is about $5. A small account is a learning account.

Where does the money lost in forex go?

To four places: fees (spread, commission and swap), the broker itself when it takes the other side of your trade, better traders and institutions, and, outside the market, scams and paid signal sellers.

How much should I risk per trade?

On a small learning account of about $50 to $500, 2–3% per trade is acceptable. On a bigger account keep it at 1% or less. After 10 losses in a row, 1% risk costs about 9.6% of the account, 20% risk costs about 89%.

Is forex a zero-sum game?

Before costs, roughly yes: money moves from one hand to another. After the spread, commission and swap are taken out, the total of all traders is negative, which is why brokers, banks and signal sellers earn whichever way the market moves.

Do Islamic swap-free accounts have no cost?

They have no swap, but most brokers charge a fixed admin fee for holding trades overnight, either after a few days or from the first day, and some widen the spread. Terms differ, so read your broker's conditions.
