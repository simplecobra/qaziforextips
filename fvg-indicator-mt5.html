What the data says

  • The edge is at the 50% (CE) line. A limit entry at the CE of a fair value gap won more often than the same trade on a random zone in all 8 tests.
  • "Price touched the gap and bounced" is not proof. On that test FVGs and random zones were almost equal (42% vs 41%). Indicators that only show a "respected %" can mislead you.
  • Trend and killzone filters did not add an edge on average. That is why QFT FVG Pro shows them as tags and measures them on your chart instead of selling "strong" stars.
  • An FVG is a zone, not a full strategy. At 1:2 you need about 34% wins to break even before costs. Combine gaps with market structure, liquidity and strict risk management.

How to trade fair value gaps with QFT FVG Pro

  1. Read the panel. Is the CE 1:2 row green on your symbol and timeframe? If FVGs do not beat random zones there, choose another timeframe or trade them with extra care.
  2. Find the context. Is price near a gold higher timeframe FVG? Which way is the H4 trend? Was a previous high or low swept?
  3. Wait for a fresh gap created by a strong move away from that area, ideally one that breaks structure.
  4. Plan at the CE line. Limit at 50%, stop just beyond the far edge, target at least 1:2 or the next liquidity. Size the trade with our lot size calculator.
  5. Respect the stop. If a candle closes straight through the gap, it becomes an inversion FVG: the idea is wrong.

What is a fair value gap (FVG)?

A fair value gap is a three-candle imbalance: a strong middle candle moves so fast that the wicks of candle 1 and candle 3 do not overlap. The empty space is the gap. ICT and Smart Money traders watch for price to return to it. Full explanation in our fair value gap guide.

What is an inversion fair value gap (IFVG)?

When price closes straight through a fair value gap, the gap can flip its role: a broken bullish gap can act as resistance and a broken bearish gap as support. QFT FVG Pro marks these as Bullish or Bearish Inversion FVG.

What is a balanced price range (BPR)?

A BPR is the overlap of a bullish and a bearish fair value gap that formed close together. QFT FVG Pro draws it in purple as Balanced Price Range (BPR).

What is the CE or 50% of an FVG?

CE (consequent encroachment) is the middle, 50% level of a fair value gap. Many ICT traders place their limit entry there. In our test of 23,773 gaps, entries at the CE line beat random zones in every market and timeframe tested.

Do fair value gaps really work?

In our test on Gold and EURUSD (2023–2025, M5 and M15), a limit at the 50% line of a fair value gap won more often than the same trade on random zones in all 8 tests (31% vs 28% at 1:2). Simply touching a gap and bouncing was not better than random. So FVGs are useful zones, not a complete strategy; combine them with structure, liquidity and risk management.

Does the FVG indicator repaint?

No. A gap is drawn only after its third candle has closed and it is never moved or removed afterwards. Only its status changes when price touches, fills or closes through it.
