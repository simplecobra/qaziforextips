Basics (10)

#

SMC

Smart Money Concepts

A way of reading the chart that looks for the footprints of large players (banks, funds): structure, liquidity and points of interest. It is an organised form of price action, not magic and not a guarantee.

Lesson 0.1: What Is SMC (Smart Money Concepts)? ›
#

ICT

The Inner Circle Trader

The teaching name of US trader Michael J. Huddleston. Much of SMC (FVG, OTE, killzones, Silver Bullet) comes from his ideas.

Lesson 0.1: What Is SMC (Smart Money Concepts)? ›
#

Smart money

Institutions and large players

Large institutions such as banks, hedge funds and market makers whose big orders move price. To fill large orders they need liquidity.

Lesson 0.1: What Is SMC (Smart Money Concepts)? ›
#

Retail trader

Individual trader

An individual trader with a small account. Retail stop losses usually sit near obvious highs and lows, which is why those places become liquidity.

Lesson 0.1: What Is SMC (Smart Money Concepts)? ›
#

Candle body

Open to close

The thick part of a candle from open to close. In SMC a break only counts when the body closes beyond the level, not just a wick.

Lesson 0.2: Candlestick Anatomy: Body, Wick and Momentum ›
#

Wick

Shadow

The thin line above or below the body. A long wick shows price went there and was rejected; it is often a sign that liquidity was taken.

Lesson 0.2: Candlestick Anatomy: Body, Wick and Momentum ›
#

Momentum candle

Large-body candle

A candle with a big body and small wicks that shows strength in one direction. Such candles create displacement and FVGs.

Lesson 0.2: Candlestick Anatomy: Body, Wick and Momentum ›
#

Swing high

Local top

A candle high with lower highs on both sides. It is the basic point of structure, and buy-side liquidity rests above it.

Lesson 0.3: Swing High and Swing Low: How to Mark Swings ›
#

Swing low

Local bottom

A candle low with higher lows on both sides. Sell-side liquidity rests below it.

Lesson 0.3: Swing High and Swing Low: How to Mark Swings ›
#

HTF / LTF

Higher timeframe / lower timeframe

The higher timeframe (such as D1, H4) gives the direction or bias; the lower timeframe (such as M15, M5) is used to confirm the entry.

Market structure (14)

#

Market structure

Trend map of highs and lows

The sequence of swing highs and lows that tells whether the trend is up, down or ranging. Every SMC decision starts here.

Lesson 1.1: Market Structure: HH, HL, LH, LL ›
#

HH

Higher high

A swing high above the previous high. In an uptrend price makes HHs and HLs.

Lesson 1.1: Market Structure: HH, HL, LH, LL ›
#

HL

Higher low

A swing low above the previous low. In an uptrend buys are usually looked for near the HL.

Lesson 1.1: Market Structure: HH, HL, LH, LL ›
#

LH

Lower high

A swing high below the previous high. In a downtrend price makes LHs and LLs.

Lesson 1.1: Market Structure: HH, HL, LH, LL ›
#

LL

Lower low

A swing low below the previous low. Each new LL in a downtrend is a BOS of the previous low.

Lesson 1.1: Market Structure: HH, HL, LH, LL ›
#

BOS

Break of structure

A body close through the previous swing in the direction of the trend. It shows the trend is continuing. A wick alone through the level is not a BOS.

Lesson 1.2: Break of Structure (BOS) ›
#

CHoCH

Change of character

The first structure break against the trend: the last HL breaking in an uptrend, or the last LH breaking in a downtrend. It is the first sign of a possible reversal, not a guarantee.

Lesson 1.3: Change of Character (CHoCH) ›
#

MSS

Market structure shift

A structure break with displacement after a liquidity sweep, usually leaving an FVG. The core of the ICT entry model.

Lesson 5.2: Model A: Sweep, MSS, FVG ›
#

Internal structure

Minor structure inside a swing

The small highs and lows that form inside a larger swing. Breaking them does not change the main trend; it is often just a pullback.

Lesson 1.4: Internal vs Swing Structure ›
#

Swing structure

Major structure

The structure of the real, major swing points that sets the trend direction. Bias is always taken from it.

Lesson 1.4: Internal vs Swing Structure ›
#

Protected high / low

Swing that must hold

The swing high or low that produced the BOS. While it holds the trend holds; when it breaks, that is a CHoCH. Stop losses often go beyond it.

Lesson 1.5: Protected High/Low and Valid Pullback ›
#

Valid pullback

Pullback that sweeps the prior candle

A pullback in which price takes the previous candle's high or low before turning. It confirms a new swing point.

Lesson 1.5: Protected High/Low and Valid Pullback ›
#

IDM

Inducement

A small high or low that forms before the real POI and traps early traders. It usually gets swept first, then price reaches the real zone.

Lesson 1.6: Inducement (IDM) ›
#

Bias

Expected direction

The expected direction, buy or sell, taken from higher timeframe structure and liquidity. On the lower timeframe you only look for entries in that direction.

Lesson 1.7: Multi-Timeframe Structure ›

Liquidity (11)

#

Liquidity

Resting orders

Places on the chart where many stop losses and pending orders sit. Large players drive price toward them to fill their own orders.

Lesson 2.1: Liquidity: BSL and SSL ›
#

BSL

Buy-side liquidity

Orders resting above highs: sellers' stop losses and breakout buyers' orders.

Lesson 2.1: Liquidity: BSL and SSL ›
#

SSL

Sell-side liquidity

Orders resting below lows: buyers' stop losses and breakout sellers' orders.

Lesson 2.1: Liquidity: BSL and SSL ›
#

EQH / EQL

Equal highs / equal lows

Two or more highs or lows at roughly the same level. Retail sees them as a double top or bottom, so extra liquidity builds there.

Lesson 2.2: Equal Highs/Lows and Trendline Liquidity ›
#

Trendline liquidity

Stops along a trendline

Stop losses gathered just beyond a clean trendline. Price often breaks the line to take them, then moves in the real direction.

Lesson 2.2: Equal Highs/Lows and Trendline Liquidity ›
#

PDH / PDL

Previous day high / low

The previous day's highest and lowest prices. They are among the most important daily liquidity levels and often become the next day's targets.

Lesson 2.3: Session Liquidity, PDH and PDL ›
#

PWH / PWL

Previous week high / low

The previous week's highest and lowest prices, used for weekly bias and larger targets.

Lesson 2.3: Session Liquidity, PDH and PDL ›
#

Asian range

Asian session high / low

The high and low of the Asian session. London often sweeps one side of this range before the day's real move starts.

Lesson 2.3: Session Liquidity, PDH and PDL ›
#

Liquidity sweep

Stop hunt / liquidity grab

Price trading beyond a high or low to take the liquidity, then closing back inside. In a break price stays beyond; in a sweep it comes back.

Lesson 2.4: Liquidity Sweep vs Break ›
#

DOL

Draw on liquidity

The liquidity price is most likely heading toward. It sets both your target and your bias.

Lesson 2.5: Draw on Liquidity ›
#

IRL / ERL

Internal / external range liquidity

Internal liquidity is the FVGs inside a range; external liquidity is the range high and low. Price usually travels from one to the other.

Lesson 2.5: Draw on Liquidity ›

POI and zones (16)

#

POI

Point of interest

A zone where a reaction is expected: an order block, FVG, breaker and so on. Not every POI is equal; score it with the checklist.

Lesson 3.7: POI Checklist ›
#

PD array

Premium/discount array

ICT's name for the set of zones (OB, FVG, breaker, liquidity) used according to whether price is in premium or discount.

Lesson 3.6: Premium, Discount and OTE ›
#

OB

Order block

The last opposite candle before a BOS: a bullish order block is the last bearish candle before the up move. When price returns, a reaction is expected there.

Lesson 3.1: Order Block ›
#

FVG

Fair value gap

A three-candle imbalance: the empty space between the first candle's wick and the third candle's wick. Price often returns to fill it.

Lesson 3.2: Fair Value Gap and IFVG ›
#

IFVG

Inverse fair value gap

An FVG that price closes through with a body; after that it works in reverse, so a support FVG becomes resistance.

Lesson 3.2: Fair Value Gap and IFVG ›
#

CE

Consequent encroachment

The middle 50% level of an FVG. Many traders use it for entry or as a check of whether the gap is holding.

Lesson 3.2: Fair Value Gap and IFVG ›
#

BISI / SIBI

Buyside imbalance sellside inefficiency / sellside imbalance buyside inefficiency

ICT names for FVGs: BISI is a bullish FVG (formed in an up move) and SIBI a bearish FVG (formed in a down move).

Lesson 3.2: Fair Value Gap and IFVG ›
#

BPR

Balanced price range

The overlapping part where a bullish and a bearish FVG cover the same prices. It is treated as a strong reaction zone.

Lesson 3.2: Fair Value Gap and IFVG ›
#

Breaker

Breaker block

An order block that breaks after a liquidity sweep; it now works in the opposite direction.

Lesson 3.3: Breaker Block ›
#

Mitigation block

Failed order block without a sweep

An order block that breaks without a sweep. Trapped traders exit to cut losses when price returns, which causes the reaction.

Lesson 3.4: Mitigation Block ›
#

Rejection block

Wick zone that took liquidity

A zone of long wicks at a high or low that took liquidity and was rejected. The zone runs from the wick tip to the body.

Lesson 3.5: Rejection and Propulsion Block ›
#

Propulsion block

Candle that propels from an order block

A new candle formed inside an order block that pushes the move forward. On a return, a reaction is expected from its upper part.

Lesson 3.5: Rejection and Propulsion Block ›
#

Premium / discount

Above / below 50% of the range

The part of a range above 50% is premium (expensive, the place to sell) and the part below is discount (cheap, the place to buy).

Lesson 3.6: Premium, Discount and OTE ›
#

Equilibrium

50% of the range

The exact 50% level of a range, which separates premium from discount.

Lesson 3.6: Premium, Discount and OTE ›
#

OTE

Optimal trade entry

The 62% to 79% pullback zone, measured with Fibonacci. In ICT's view the best entry price sits here.

Lesson 3.6: Premium, Discount and OTE ›
#

Displacement

Strong, fast move

A fast move of big-bodied candles that leaves an FVG behind. It is the clearest sign that large players have stepped in.

Lesson 5.5: Confirmation vs Risk Entry ›

Time and sessions (7)

#

Sessions

Asian, London and New York

The three main parts of the trading day: Asian, London and New York. Each behaves differently; most movement comes in London and New York.

Lesson 4.1: Sessions and Killzones ›
#

Killzone

Peak activity window

The specific hours of a session when large players are most active (London and New York killzones). Pakistan-time hours are in lesson 4.1.

Lesson 4.1: Sessions and Killzones ›
#

Silver Bullet

One-hour ICT setup

ICT's model inside a specific one-hour window: a liquidity sweep, an FVG forming and an entry from that FVG, all within the hour.

Lesson 4.2: Silver Bullet ›
#

AMD / PO3

Accumulation, manipulation, distribution (Power of 3)

The three phases of a day or a candle: accumulation (range), manipulation (a false move and sweep), then distribution (the real move).

Lesson 4.3: Power of 3 (AMD) ›
#

Judas swing

False move of the day

The early false move of the day that pulls traders the wrong way and takes liquidity before price runs in the real direction. It is the manipulation part of AMD.

Lesson 4.3: Power of 3 (AMD) ›
#

Midnight open

New York 00:00 opening price

The price at 00:00 New York time. ICT treats it as the day's reference level: better buys sit below it and better sells above it.

Lesson 4.3: Power of 3 (AMD) ›
#

High-impact news

NFP, CPI, FOMC

News such as NFP, CPI or FOMC that moves price hard within seconds and widens spreads. Our rule: no new trade 30 minutes before or after.

Lesson 4.4: Trading Around News ›

Entries (5)

#

Top-down analysis

From higher to lower timeframe

Bias and liquidity from the higher timeframe first, then the POI from the middle timeframe, and finally entry confirmation on the lower timeframe.

Lesson 5.1: Top-Down Analysis ›
#

Confirmation entry

Entry after LTF confirmation

Waiting for a sweep and CHoCH on the lower timeframe after price reaches the POI, then entering. Higher win chance, but some trades are missed.

Lesson 5.5: Confirmation vs Risk Entry ›
#

Risk entry

Limit order at the POI

A limit order straight at the POI without confirmation. It gives a better RR but more trades get stopped out.

Lesson 5.5: Confirmation vs Risk Entry ›
#

CRT

Candle range theory

Price sweeps one edge of the previous (usually H4 or D1) candle and closes back inside its range; the target is then the other edge of that range.

Lesson 5.4: Models C, D and E ›
#

Entry model

Fixed set of entry rules

A written, fixed set of entry steps, such as Model A: sweep, then MSS, then entry from the FVG. Master one model and backtest it.

Lesson 5.2: Model A: Sweep, MSS, FVG ›

Risk and management (13)

#

Stop loss

SL

The price at which the trade closes automatically at a loss. In SMC it goes beyond the swept high or low or the POI, where your idea is proven wrong.

Lesson 6.1: Risk Rules and Stop Loss ›
#

Take profit

TP

The price where profit is taken. In SMC the target is usually the next liquidity: the opposite swing, PDH or PDL, or equal highs and lows.

Lesson 6.2: Take Profit and Trade Management ›
#

R

One unit of risk

The amount risked on one trade is 1R. Results are counted in it: +2R means twice the risk in profit, −1R means a full stop.

Lesson 6.1: Risk Rules and Stop Loss ›
#

RR

Reward-to-risk ratio

The ratio of potential profit to risk. Our rule is at least 1:2: a target of 2 for every 1 risked.

Lesson 6.1: Risk Rules and Stop Loss ›
#

Lot size

Position size

The size of the trade. It is calculated from the stop-loss distance and your fixed risk, never guessed.

Lesson 0.4: Timeframes, Lot Size and 1% Risk ›
#

1% rule

Risk per trade

Risk at most 1% of the account on any trade. That way even ten losses in a row cannot wipe out the account.

Lesson 0.4: Timeframes, Lot Size and 1% Risk ›
#

Break even

BE

Moving the stop loss to the entry price so the trade cannot close at a loss. Done too early, good trades also get closed at zero.

Lesson 6.2: Take Profit and Trade Management ›
#

Partial close

Taking part of the profit

Closing part of the trade at the first target to lock in profit and letting the rest run to the next target.

Lesson 6.2: Take Profit and Trade Management ›
#

Drawdown

Fall from equity peak

How far the account has fallen from its highest point. Set daily and weekly limits; when one is hit, stop trading for that period.

Lesson 6.1: Risk Rules and Stop Loss ›
#

Backtesting

Testing rules on past charts

Testing your written rules on past charts candle by candle. At least 100 backtest trades are needed before going live.

Lesson 7.1: Backtesting ›
#

Win rate

Percentage of winning trades

The percentage of trades that win. Win rate alone is not enough; read it with RR. A 40% win rate can still be profitable at 1:2.

Lesson 7.1: Backtesting ›
#

Expectancy

Average R per trade

The average R won or lost per trade. If it is positive the system has an edge; if negative, change the rules, do not raise the risk.

Lesson 7.1: Backtesting ›
#

Trading journal

Record of every trade

A record of every trade: screenshot, reason, model, result and your state of mind at the time. It is how you catch your own mistakes.

Lesson 7.2: Trading Journal ›

Questions

Which SMC terms should I learn first?

Start with swing highs and lows, HH, HL, LH and LL, then BOS and CHoCH. Without them liquidity and order blocks will not make sense.

What is the difference between BOS and CHoCH?

A BOS is a swing break in the trend direction (the trend continues); a CHoCH is the first break against the trend (the first sign of a reversal).

Are ICT and SMC the same thing?

Mostly. Many SMC terms (FVG, OTE, killzone, Silver Bullet) come from ICT; SMC is a simplified, organised name for the same ideas.
