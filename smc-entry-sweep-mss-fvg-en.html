Model A: Sweep, MSS, FVG

Model A is the best-known and cleanest SMC entry model: first a liquidity sweep, then a market structure shift (MSS), then an entry on the return into the FVG. In this lesson you will learn its full rules, checklist, bullish and bearish examples, and how to recognise it failing.

Model A at a glance

  1. Sweep: price takes clear liquidity (the Asian low, PDL, equal lows).
  2. MSS: right after the sweep, the last minor high breaks with strong displacement.
  3. FVG: that displacement leaves a fair value gap.
  4. Entry: when price returns into the FVG.
  5. SL: below the swept low. TP: liquidity on the other side.

This is often called the ICT "2022 model". You already know every part: the sweep (lesson 2.4), CHoCH and MSS (lesson 1.3), the FVG (lesson 3.2).

Full rules

Bullish model A

  • Condition 1, bias: H4 or H1 bias is bullish, or price is in higher timeframe discount and inside a major POI.
  • Condition 2, sweep: on M5 or M15, a wick or brief move below a clear low, then back.
  • Condition 3, MSS: the move after the sweep breaks the last minor high before the sweep with a body close.
  • Condition 4, displacement and FVG: the MSS move has large-bodied candles and leaves an FVG.
  • Condition 5, time: all of this in a killzone, with no high-impact news in the next 30 minutes.
  • Entry: a limit at the top edge of the FVG or its CE (50%).
  • SL: a little below the swept low.
  • TP1: the nearby high (the MSS high or session high); TP2: the draw on liquidity.
  • No trade if the RR is below 1:2.

Bearish model A

Everything reversed: a sweep of a clear high, a body break of the last minor low, a bearish FVG, a sell on the return into the FVG, SL above the swept high, TP at the sell-side liquidity below.

Why model A works

  • Sweep: traders on the wrong side got trapped, and their stops filled the large player's orders.
  • MSS: the large player is now positioned in the real direction, because minor structure broke forcefully.
  • FVG: price often returns to fill the gap the large order left; that is our cheaper entry.
  • The SL is logical: if the swept low breaks too, the sweep failed and the idea is over.

Good vs weak model A

When model A fails

  • Price enters the FVG and closes a body below it: weakness. If you are not filled yet, cancel the limit.
  • Price closes below the swept low: the model failed. This often happens when the higher timeframe bias is opposite and the "sweep" was really a break.
  • A model A formed at news often sweeps both sides; avoid news.
RememberAfter every model A trade ask: "Which liquidity did the sweep take?" If you cannot name it (Asian low, PDL, EQL), the setup was weak.

Gold example

Tuesday, winter. H4 bullish, DOL at PDH 4,148.

  • Asia: range 4,121 to 4,130.
  • London open: price dropped to 4,113, sweeping the Asian low (4,121).
  • Next 15 minutes: large green M5 candles broke the 4,128 minor high (MSS) and left an FVG from 4,121 to 4,127.
  • Return: price came back to 4,124 (the CE). Entry 4,124, SL 4,111, risk $13.
  • TP1: the post-sweep high 4,134 ($10). TP2: PDH 4,148 ($24, about 1:1.8).
  • That is below our 1:2 minimum. A better entry: a limit at the lower part of the FVG at 4,122, SL 4,111, risk $11, $26 to TP2: 1:2.4. Follow the rule, not the excitement.
  • At 1% risk on a $1,000 account ($10): 10 ÷ (11 × 100) = 0.009, below 0.01 lot. Either skip the trade or accept that 0.01 lot means about 1.1% risk.

Common mistakes

Common mistakeEntering on the sweep without an MSS. Price can keep falling after a sweep; the MSS shows the large player has turned.
Common mistakeA market order on the MSS candle. Expensive entry and a big stop; wait for the FVG retrace.
Common mistakeModel A against the higher timeframe. M5 prints several "model A"s a day; take only those with the bias.
Common mistakeForcing a smaller stop when the RR is low. The stop comes from logic; RR is the result. If it is too low, skip.

Summary

  • Model A: a sweep of clear liquidity, an MSS with displacement, an entry in the FVG.
  • SL beyond the sweep, TP at opposite liquidity, RR at least 1:2.
  • Only with higher timeframe bias and in a killzone.
  • If the swept low breaks, the model failed.

Questions

What is the sweep, MSS, FVG model?

A liquidity sweep, then a market structure shift with displacement, then an entry on the retrace into the fair value gap, with the stop beyond the sweep.

Is this the ICT 2022 model?

Yes, it is commonly called the ICT 2022 mentorship model.

Which timeframe is used?

Entries on M1 to M15, bias from H1 or H4.
