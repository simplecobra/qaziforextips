Herd mentality in forex trading: why following the crowd costs beginners money

One sheep jumped off a cliff and 1,500 followed it. Dr. Qazi uses that true story to explain the most expensive habit in trading: copying what everyone else does, without your own practice, rules or anyone to correct your mistakes.

Written by Dr. Qazi Abdul Basit, Ph.D, in the Qazi ForeX Tips group
First posted in the group 10 July 2021 · Published here 10 November 2026
The question behind this post

YouTube videos dekh kar ya kisi ka course le kar trading shuru ki, phir loss ho gaya. Kya forex dhoka hai, ya ghalti hamari hai?

چند سال پہلے ترکی میں ایک بھیڑ نے نجانے کیوں پہاڑی سے چھلانگ لگا دی، اور اس کی دیکھا دیکھی پندرہ سو مزید بھیڑوں نے چھلانگ لگائی۔ چار سو پچاس بھیڑیں موت کی بھینٹ چڑھ گئیں۔ لاشوں کا ڈھیر اونچا ہونے کی وجہ سے باقی یا بچ گئیں یا زخمی ہو گئیں۔ اسے کہتے ہیں ہرڈ سائیکالوجی (Herd Psychology)، یعنی ریوڑ کی نفسیات، اور عام زبان میں بھیڑ چال۔

ہرڈ تھیوری کا مطلب ہے کسی ایک کے عمل کو پورا گروہ فالو کرے، بغیر کسی براہِ راست ہدایت کے۔ یہ زیادہ تر جانوروں میں دیکھا جاتا ہے، جیسے مچھلیوں کا ایک سمت تیرنا، پرندوں کا اڑنا یا بھیڑوں کا چلنا۔ مگر انسانوں میں بھی یہ عمل اس وقت بہت دیکھا جاتا ہے جب ہم اندھا دھند کسی کو فالو کرنے لگ جاتے ہیں۔

میں اس بات کو فاریکس کے ساتھ جوڑنا چاہوں گا۔ جب آپ یوٹیوب سے ویڈیوز دیکھ کر یا کسی کا کورس لے کر ٹریڈنگ شروع کر دیتے ہیں، اور پھر لاس کر کے سوچتے ہیں کہ فاریکس ایک دھوکا ہے۔

ایک بات یاد رکھیں: جب تک آپ پریکٹس نہیں کریں گے، کامیاب نہیں ہوں گے۔ اور صرف پریکٹس سے بھی کام نہیں چلے گا۔ کامیابی اس وقت ملے گی جب آپ کے پریکٹس ورک میں سے کوئی غلطیاں نکالنے والا ہو اور آپ کی تصحیح بھی کرے۔ کہتے ہیں کہ انسان غلطیوں سے سیکھتا ہے، لیکن سیکھتا اس وقت ہے جب کوئی اسے بتائے کہ بھائی یہ جو کر رہے ہو غلط ہے، ٹھیک اس طرح ہے۔ پھر وہ غلطی دوبارہ نہیں ہوتی۔

بہت سے لوگ گروپ میں چپ رہتے ہیں، حالانکہ یہاں تصحیح کرنے والا مفت میں موجود ہے۔ اپنا کام شیئر کریں، فائدہ آپ کا ہی ہے۔ تمام لوگ اپنے چارٹس اور تجزیے شیئر کیا کریں، اور اگر کسی کے چارٹ میں کوئی غلطی نظر آئے تو اپنے علم کے مطابق اس پر رائے ضرور دیں۔

The true story: 1,500 sheep, one cliff

In July 2005, near the town of Gevaş in Van province, eastern Turkey, one sheep jumped off a cliff while its shepherds were having breakfast. Nearly 1,500 others followed it. About 450 died; the rest survived only because the pile of sheep below broke their fall. None of them saw a reason to jump. They saw other sheep jumping.

Psychologists call this herd behaviour (also the bandwagon effect): people copy what a group is doing because the group is doing it, not because they have checked it themselves. In Urdu we simply say bhed chaal.

What herd mentality looks like in forex

  • The "secret strategy" video. A YouTube video shows a few winning trades. Thousands copy it on a live account the same week, without testing it once.
  • Signal and copy groups. Everyone enters the same pair at the same price because one person posted "BUY GOLD NOW". Nobody knows why, so nobody knows when it is wrong. See forex signals in Pakistan: what they don't tell you.
  • Chasing a move that already happened. Gold has risen 30 dollars, the group is excited, and you buy at the top because everyone is bullish.
  • Panic exits. The group panics on a red candle, so you close a good trade that your plan said to hold.
  • "Everyone uses this indicator." Popularity is not an edge. If an indicator or setup is popular, ask what it actually measures and test it yourself.

Why the crowd usually loses

When most traders are on one side, three things tend to be true:

  1. They enter late. By the time a move is popular on social media, much of it has already happened. Late entries carry the most risk of a reversal.
  2. Their stops sit in the same places. Crowded trades put stop losses at the same obvious highs and lows. Those clusters are exactly where liquidity sits, and price often sweeps them before moving on.
  3. They have no exit plan. If you entered because others did, you will also exit because others did. That is how a small loss becomes a washed account.

Going against the crowd every time is not the answer either. That is still letting the crowd decide for you. The answer is your own plan.

Practice is not enough: you need someone to correct you

This is the heart of Dr. Qazi's post. Most people who say "forex is a scam" after a loss did one of two things: they skipped practice completely, or they practised alone and repeated the same mistakes hundreds of times.

A mistake only teaches you when someone shows you why it was a mistake and what the right way looks like. That is why every chart in the Qazi ForeX Tips group must come with its reasoning, and why Dr. Qazi and other members comment on it. Posting your work feels uncomfortable at first. It is also the fastest way to stop repeating errors.

If you practise on your own, make your mistakes visible: keep a trading journal and review it every week, and replay past charts in the QFT Replay Simulator before risking real money.

5 ways to stop following the herd

  1. Analyse first, read opinions later. Mark your trend, levels and entry before you open any group, channel or video.
  2. Write the plan before the trade. Entry, stop loss, take profit and risk in money. If you cannot write it, you are following someone.
  3. Ask one question: "Would I take this trade if nobody else was talking about it?" If the answer is no, skip it.
  4. Back-test anything you copy. A strategy from a video is only an idea until you have tested it on many past setups. Use a stop loss on every trade.
  5. Get your work checked. Share your chart with your reasons in the group or in a checked practice batch, and actually change what you are told is wrong.

So, is forex a scam?

A loss after copying a video does not prove that the market is fake. It usually proves that the strategy was used without practice, rules and risk control. Real scams do exist: people who promise fixed monthly profits, ask for your account login, or only show winning screenshots. Judge every offer on its own, and judge your own trading by your journal, not by the crowd's mood. Read more about the mind side of trading in our trading psychology guide.

Questions

What is herd mentality in forex trading?

It is following what most traders are doing, such as buying because everyone is buying, without your own analysis, plan or risk limits.

Why is following the crowd dangerous in trading?

The crowd usually enters late, places stop losses at the same obvious levels that price often sweeps, and has no exit plan, so small losses grow.

Is forex a scam if I lost money after watching YouTube videos?

A loss after copying videos does not prove forex is a scam; it usually shows the strategy was used without practice, rules and risk control. Scams do exist, such as fixed-profit offers, so judge each offer separately.

How do I stop following the herd in trading?

Do your own analysis before reading opinions, write your plan before entering, ask whether you would take the trade alone, back-test what you copy, use a stop loss and get your charts reviewed.
