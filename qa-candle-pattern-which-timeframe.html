Which timeframe should you judge a candle pattern on?

A member asked which timeframe candle patterns should be judged on. Dr. Qazi's rule is short: judge the pattern on the same timeframe as your level, then go smaller only for the entry.

Answered by Dr. Qazi Abdul Basit, Ph.D, in the Qazi ForeX Tips group
Published 12 November 2026
The question from the group

In candle patterns ki judgement kis time frame par karni chahiye?

The rule: same timeframe as your level

A candle pattern means something only where it forms. A pin bar in the middle of nowhere is noise; the same pin bar at a daily support level is information. So judge the pattern on the timeframe your level was drawn on. If the level comes from the daily or H4 chart, the candle that matters is the daily or H4 candle at that level.

Step by step

  1. Mark the level on the higher timeframe (daily or H4): support, resistance, supply or demand. See types of levels.
  2. Read the candle at the level on that same timeframe. Is it a rejection candle (long wick, price pushed away) or a momentum candle (big body, price pushing through)? Rejection suggests the level holds; momentum suggests it may break.
  3. Go down to H1 or M15 for the entry and wait for a confirmation candle in the direction of the higher-timeframe signal.

This is the core of multi-timeframe analysis: the big timeframe gives the location and the signal, the small one gives the timing.

Why not decide on M1 or M5?

Very small timeframes contain a lot of noise. Patterns form and fail constantly, spreads matter more, and fake breakouts are common. An M5 engulfing candle against a clear daily rejection is usually just a ripple. Use M1 and M5 only to fine-tune an entry that the higher timeframe already supports.

Bigger timeframe, stronger signal

A daily candle represents a full day of buying and selling by every participant; an M1 candle represents one minute. That is why the same pattern carries more weight on higher timeframes. The practice to build: level and pattern on the big timeframe, entry on the small one. Questions

Which timeframe is best for candlestick patterns?

Judge the pattern on the timeframe your level comes from, usually daily or H4, and use H1 or M15 only for the entry.

Do candle patterns work on M1 and M5?

They are unreliable on their own there because of noise and fake breakouts. Use small timeframes only to refine an entry the higher timeframe supports.

Why are higher-timeframe candles stronger?

Each candle contains more trading activity, so a rejection or momentum candle on daily or H4 reflects a bigger shift than one on M1.

What should I look for at a level?

Whether the candle at the level is a rejection candle, suggesting the level holds, or a momentum candle, suggesting it may break.
