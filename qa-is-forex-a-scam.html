Is forex a scam? Why shortcuts fail, and the 4 rules that make it a real skill

"Forex is a scam" is something almost every beginner hears. After more than a decade of trading, Dr. Qazi's answer is simple: the market is real, the shortcut is the scam. Here is why, where the real scams are, and the four rules he says no trader can skip.

Why so many people say forex is a scam

Most people who call forex a scam had one of three experiences: they lost money copying a video or a signal, they gave their account to someone who promised fixed monthly profit, or they used an unregulated broker. All three are real losses. None of them prove that the currency market itself is fake.

What forex actually is

Forex is the exchange of one country's currency for another's. Banks, importers, exporters, airlines, investment funds and central banks do it every day for real business reasons, which is why it is the largest financial market in the world. As long as countries and their currencies exist, someone needs to exchange them. That part is not a scam.

What is true is that most retail traders lose money. Brokers in many countries are required to show the percentage of retail accounts that lose on CFDs, and that figure is usually well above half. That is a warning about how people trade, not proof that the market is fake.

Where the real scams are

  • Fixed profit promises. Anyone who guarantees a monthly return in forex is either lying or taking risks you cannot see.
  • Account managers. Giving your login to someone else to "trade for you" is the most common way people lose everything. See why you should not trade, or hand over, someone else's account.
  • Signal and strategy sellers. Winning screenshots prove nothing; read forex signals in Pakistan: what they don't tell you.
  • Unregulated brokers. A broker that delays withdrawals or plays games with prices can turn a good trade into a loss. Check any broker first: how to check a forex broker.

The 4 rules Dr. Qazi says no trader can skip

  1. Control your emotions. Fear closes good trades early and greed holds bad ones too long. Rules and a written plan remove most of those decisions: trading psychology guide.
  2. Money management with a daily target. Decide how much you risk per trade and what a good day looks like. When the target is reached, stop. Without limits, no account survives for long: money management plan.
  3. Only take trades where the reward is at least twice the risk. No method wins 100% of the time. With 1:2, you can be wrong on more than half your trades and still grow the account: risk to reward ratio explained.
  4. Make price action your base. You can add indicators or other methods, but the decision should come from what price is doing at your levels: price action trading guide.

And a fifth rule that sits above all of them: never give your account to anyone to manage.

Questions

Is forex a scam?

No. Forex is the exchange of one currency for another, done every day by banks, companies and funds. Scams exist around it, such as fixed-profit offers, account managers and unregulated brokers, so judge each offer separately.

Why do most people lose money in forex?

Most beginners trade without practice, without a stop loss and without a risk limit, often copying signals or videos. The market then punishes the shortcut, not the skill.

What are the most important rules for forex trading?

Control your emotions, manage money with a fixed risk and a daily target, only take trades with at least 1:2 reward to risk, and base decisions on price action.

Should I give my trading account to someone to manage?

No. Handing over your login is one of the most common ways people lose their whole account.
